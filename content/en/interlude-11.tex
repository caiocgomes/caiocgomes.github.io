\bookinterlude{III}{IV}{Who Gets the Key?}
Until Friday, the phone always rang after someone had picked the lathe. I'd consult the forest, count how many trees were betting the part would come out the right size, and pass the prediction along. On Friday the phone rings earlier. It's the foreman with the bar in his hand. “Which one do I put it in?”

On the lathe by the window, nine parts out of ten have come out the right size. The one by the door came back from the repair shop yesterday. The customer is picking up one part today and has ordered another hundred just like it for Monday. We'll use the same settings both days.

I turn to a fresh page in the logbook and draw two columns, one for each lathe. If I send the bar to the one by the window, by this afternoon I'll know whether it got one more right. The column for the lathe by the door will still be blank. I can ask ten more people for their best guess. To enter a measurement in that column, someone is going to have to use a bar on that lathe.

I leave the key with you. Where will you put Friday's bar? While you're thinking, the phone rings again. The customer has canceled Monday's hundred parts. How much would you still pay to find out today how the repaired lathe is running?
