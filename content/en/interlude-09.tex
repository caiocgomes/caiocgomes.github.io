\bookinterlude{II}{III}{The Question in Pencil}
We order a bushing from the shop across the yard. The slip comes back with a question in pencil. “Which lathe is the part coming off?” Yesterday that would have made me laugh. We only had one.

On our lathe, settings 1 and 2 on the handwheel gave us parts measuring 5 and 6 mm. On theirs, the same settings gave them 9 and 8 mm, in that order. I pool the slips and fit a least-squares line to predict diameter from the setting. It predicts 7 mm at both settings. Nobody made a 7 mm part.

I draw one of the four slips at random. You're on the phone, trying to find out whether the part fits through a 7 mm hole. “Was it on setting 1?” A yes leaves you with 5 or 9 mm. A no leaves you with 6 or 8. Start with the question in pencil and one answer tells you whether it fits. If you also want the diameter, save the question about the handwheel for later.

Tomorrow the logbooks from eight shops are coming in, with columns for the material, the shift, and the last tool change. When the phone rings, how will you use yesterday's slips to choose today's first question?
