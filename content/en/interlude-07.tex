\bookinterlude{I}{II}{On the Other Side of the Gate}
Remember the 5.2 mm part the inspector measured twice? Let's follow it. The gate clicks, the part drops into drawer A, and I walk around the bench to see who comes to empty the drawer.

It's the woman who makes the bushings these parts have to fit into. Tomorrow she needs to bore the hole for the next bushing while our part is still on the lathe. She asks what diameter to expect. I tell her I'm willing to bet money the part will go into A. She puts a finger on the drawing. “How many millimeters do I write here?”

The inspector looks up the setting he plans to use tomorrow. The last three times he used it, two parts came out at 5.2 mm and one at 5.8 mm. All three went into A. You suggest 5.2. I take the average and suggest 5.4. On those three parts you would have been exactly right twice and off by six tenths on the third. I would have missed all three, two by two tenths and one by four.

She's still holding the pencil. Before I give her a number, I'd like to hear from the person who has to bore the holes. Whose mistakes would take more work to put right at this bench, my three or your one?
