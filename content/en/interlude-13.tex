\bookinterlude{IV}{V}{The Note on the Table}
Six months later a customer writes, “Make me another one like the one I sent back for polishing.” The card by the phone tells me which lathe is free, the last diameter we shipped, and the cost of a day's delay. Yesterday I could use it to decide whether to pay overtime. What size will you set the lathe for today?

I open her folder. The first part measured 5.2 mm, the second 5.8. The polishing stamp is on the second slip. I copy the folder and move the stamp to the first slip. The same lathe is free. The last delivery still measured 5.8 mm. This time she expects a 5.2 mm part.

I'm tempted to add “last part polished” to the card. I do. The next morning she asks for a part like the first one she approved after moving to her new address. I open the folder again.

On the first day there was room to spare for two coins on this table. Today I spread out the slips and lay her note in the middle. You read two lines, look around, and put your finger on one slip. Which words in the note sent you to that one?
